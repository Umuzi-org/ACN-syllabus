## KT0301: Use Scientific Notation for Small and Large Numbers

**What is Scientific Notation?**

Scientific notation is a method of representing very large or very small numbers in a compact form. It expresses a number as a product of a coefficient (between 1 and 10) and a power of 10.

**Example:**

650,000,000=6.5×108650,000,000 = 6.5 \times 10^8
0.00000000000000000000000000166=1.66×10−270.00000000000000000000000000166 = 1.66 \times 10^{-27}

**Key Concepts**

1. **Order of Magnitude**: The exponent on the 10 shows the number’s relative size compared to others.
2. **Reduction of Zeros**: Scientific notation eliminates the need to write excessive zeros.

**Examples:**
- The age of the Earth: 4,600,000,0004,600,000,000 years → 4.6×1094.6 \times 10^9 years
- Mass of one atomic mass unit (amu): 0.000000000000000000000000001660.00000000000000000000000000166 kg → 1.66×10−271.66 \times 10^{-27} kg

**Negative Exponents**

Negative exponents represent very small numbers where the decimal point moves to the left. For example:
- 1.66×10−271.66 \times 10^{-27}: Move the decimal 27 places to the left to write the number in standard form.

## KT0302: Prefixes – From Giga to Pica (10⁹ to 10⁻¹²)

**What Are Prefixes?**

Prefixes are added to units of measure to represent large or small magnitudes. They make expressing size and magnitude simpler and more standardized.

## KT0303: Conversions – SI to Imperial: Degrees F to Degrees C

**What Are Conversions?**

Conversions are used to express measurements in different units. They require a **conversion factor**, which is a multiplier or ratio used to change units.

**Key Conversion Formula: Fahrenheit to Celsius**

To convert degrees Fahrenheit (°F) to degrees Celsius (°C):
°C=(°F−32)1.8°C = \frac{(°F - 32)}{1.8}

**Example:** Convert 98.6°F to °C:

°C=(98.6−32)1.8=37°C°C = \frac{(98.6 - 32)}{1.8} = 37°C
