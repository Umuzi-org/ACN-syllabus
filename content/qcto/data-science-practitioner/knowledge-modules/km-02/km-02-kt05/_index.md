**Detailed Breakdown of Learning Outcomes**
## KT0501: Definition
   - A Cartesian coordinate system specifies each point uniquely by a pair of numerical coordinates.
   - These coordinates are the signed distances to the point from two fixed perpendicular oriented lines (x-axis and y-axis), measured in the same unit of length.
## KT0502: Terminology
- **Definition Example:**


   - The Cartesian system uses two axes, the **x-axis** (horizontal) and the **y-axis** (vertical).
   - Example Equation: 2x+y=22x + y = 2 represents a line on this system.
- **Labels:**


   - **x-axis**: Horizontal (abscissa).
   - **y-axis**: Vertical (ordinate).
   - In 3D, a third axis (z-axis) is added.

## KT0503: The Coordinate Plane
- A two-dimensional plane formed by intersecting the x-axis and y-axis perpendicularly at the **origin (0,0)**.


- The plane divides into **four quadrants**:
  - Quadrant I: Positive xx, Positive yy.
  - Quadrant II: Negative xx, Positive yy.
  - Quadrant III: Negative xx, Negative yy.
  - Quadrant IV: Positive xx, Negative yy.
- **Plotting Points:**
  - Example: Plot (1,3)(1, 3) by moving 1 unit right and 3 units up.

## KT0504: Intersecting Axes
- Axes are perpendicular, dividing the plane into four quadrants.
- The origin is at (0,0)(0, 0).
- **Ordered Pairs**: (x,y)(x, y), where xx is the horizontal position and yy is the vertical position.


## KT0505: Four Quadrants
- Each quadrant is identified based on the sign of coordinates:

  - Quadrant I: (+x,+y)(+x, +y).
  - Quadrant II: (−x,+y)(-x, +y).
  - Quadrant III: (−x,−y)(-x, -y).
  - Quadrant IV: (+x,−y)(+x, -y).

- **Examples:**


  - (1,4)(1, 4) → Quadrant I.
  - (−3,−3)(-3, -3) → Quadrant III.
## KT0506: Naming using Roman Numerals
- Roman numerals (I, II, III, IV) denote the quadrants.
- Note: Roman numerals are not applied in other unrelated contexts, such as naming ionic compounds with single cation charges.
