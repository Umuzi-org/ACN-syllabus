## KT0401: Rational and Irrational Numbers
- **Definition:**

  - **Rational Numbers**: Numbers that can be expressed as a fraction (e.g., pq\frac{p}{q}, q≠0q \neq 0). Examples include 12,−7,3.75\frac{1}{2}, -7, 3.75.
  - **Irrational Numbers**: Numbers that cannot be expressed as a fraction. These are non-terminating, non-repeating decimals (e.g., 2,π,e\sqrt{2}, \pi, e).
- **Key Differences:**

  - Rational numbers have finite or repeating decimal representations.
  - Irrational numbers have infinite, non-repeating decimals.
- **Examples:**

  - Rational: 34,7\frac{3}{4}, 7.
  - Irrational: π=3.14159...\pi = 3.14159..., 2\sqrt{2}.

## KT0402: Repeating Decimals to Fractions
- **Steps to Convert:**

  1. Let xx equal the repeating decimal.
  2. Identify the repeating digit(s).
  3. Multiply xx by the appropriate power of 10 to align the repeating digits.
  4. Subtract equations to isolate xx.
  5. Solve for xx by dividing.

- **Example 1**: 0.555...0.555...
 x=0.555...x = 0.555...
 10x=5.555...10x = 5.555...
 Subtract: 10x−x=9x=510x - x = 9x = 5
 x=59x = \frac{5}{9}.


- **Example 2:** 1.042424...1.042424...

 1000x=1042.424...1000x = 1042.424...
 10x=10.424...10x = 10.424...

 x=1032990=344330=172165x = \frac{1032}{990} = \frac{344}{330} = \frac{172}{165}.


## KT0403: Symbols for Irrational Numbers
- **Definition**: Numbers that cannot be expressed as pq\frac{p}{q}, where pp and qq are integers, and q≠0q \neq 0. Examples: 2,3,π\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \pi.


- **Properties:**


  1. Adding a rational number to an irrational number gives an irrational number.
  2. Multiplying an irrational number by a non-zero rational number gives an irrational number.
  3. The product of two irrational numbers may be rational (e.g., 2×2=2\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2).
   
## KT0404: Rounding Prematurely in Calculations
- **Definition**: A rounding error occurs when a number is altered to have fewer decimal places, potentially introducing inaccuracies.


- **Key Points:**


  - Rounding errors can compound across calculations.
  - Avoid rounding prematurely to maintain precision.
  
## KT0405: Accuracy
- **Definitions:**

  - **Accuracy**: How close a measurement is to the true value.
  - **Precision**: Consistency or closeness of repeated measurements.
- **Example:**

  - Measuring a length as 10.2 cm10.2 \, \text{cm} when the true length is 10.0 cm10.0 \, \text{cm} demonstrates accuracy.
  - Repeatedly measuring 10.2,10.21,10.19 cm10.2, 10.21, 10.19 \, \text{cm} shows precision.
