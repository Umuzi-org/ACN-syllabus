## KT0601: Definition
- **The Pythagorean Theorem** states that in a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (cc) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (aa and bb): c2=a2+b2c^2 = a^2 + b^2
- It provides the foundation for solving geometric problems and is pivotal in trigonometry, algebra, and higher-dimensional mathematics.
  
## KT0602: Terminology
- **Right Triangle:** A triangle with one angle measuring 90 degrees.
- **Hypotenuse:** The longest side of a right triangle, opposite the right angle.
- **Equation:** A mathematical statement that expresses equality (c2=a2+b2c^2 = a^2 + b^2).
- **Coordinate System:** A system for defining a point’s location using ordered pairs (x,yx, y) or triplets (x,y,zx, y, z).
- **Dimensional:** Referring to spatial attributes in one (1D), two (2D), or three dimensions (3D).
- **Formula:** A mathematical expression, e.g., (x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}, for finding the distance between two points.
- **Pythagoras:** The Greek mathematician who developed this fundamental theorem.

## KT0603: Purpose
- The theorem demonstrates the geometric relationship between the areas of squares formed by a right triangle’s sides.
- When applied to the Cartesian grid, it facilitates:
  - Finding the shortest path (distance) between two points.
  - Solving practical problems in navigation, mapping, and computer graphics.

## KT0604: Determine the Distance Between Two Points on the Cartesian Grid
1. **Concept:**
   - The Cartesian grid comprises perpendicular axes (xx and yy) with points represented as ordered pairs (x,yx, y).
   - The distance formula is derived from the Pythagorean Theorem: d=(x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
2. **Example:**
   - Find the distance between A(3,4)A(3, 4) and B(7,1)B(7, 1): d=(7−3)2+(1−4)2d = \sqrt{(7 - 3)^2 + (1 - 4)^2} d=42+(−3)2d = \sqrt{4^2 + (-3)^2} d=16+9=25=5d = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5
